## Strateos Inc — Software Engineer III

*Menlo Park, CA · Jul 2021 – Jun 2023*

- Strateos was a cloud lab startup where scientists could submit experiments through a browser and have robots carry them out. I worked on the software between those two sides—turning what a scientist wanted to do into instructions that lab equipment could execute. That helped reduce the manual time scientists spent by about 90%.

- Strateos had two labs, each with several workcells containing multiple connected devices. I worked on integrating those devices and the workflow software that coordinated them, so they could carry out the steps of an experiment together. It meant understanding both the software workflows and how the equipment operated within each workcell.

- Another project I particularly enjoyed was an automated colony-picking system that I built with one other developer. We worked with a robotic arm, a camera, image segmentation (OpenCV), and a convolutional neural network (CNN) to identify colonies and guide the arm to pick them. It brought computer vision and physical movement together, making the process about 10 times faster than doing it by hand.

- Being in a startup meant wearing multiple hats. I worked across hardware, device drivers, software, DevOps, and cloud systems, handling development, deployment, troubleshooting, and much more. It was fun to see how all those pieces came together to run an experiment. I found that very engaging and satisfying—building something that could reduce repetitive lab work, help scientists move their research forward, and reduce the drug discovery time from years to months. 

- **It is the kind of work I would love to do again.**

## Center On Access Technology, RIT/NTID — ML Software Engineer (Part-time)

*Rochester, NY · Jan 2021 – May 2021*

- The NTID lab was exploring a hybrid captioning system for classrooms with students who are deaf or hard of hearing. Instead of a captioner typing everything as the teacher spoke, the idea was to have a speech-to-text model generate the right captions given the context, with the captioner correcting places where it got things wrong.

- A friend and I worked on this project using the speech-to-text data we were given. We tried n-gram approaches as part of that work and later came up with the idea of building language and error models to help improve the captions. We also explored models on Hugging Face, including BERT and RoBERTa, to understand how they performed on the data and what might be useful for the system.

- We also built a sample UI and backend services so we could run experiments, deploy different model versions, and compare the resulting captions. This helped us see what changed between versions and whether those changes improved the output.
